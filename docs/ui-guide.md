# Tilemaxx UI guide

## Game workspace

Choose **1 player · Solo** in **New game** to practice on your own. Type your
rack, then use **Find moves** for suggestions or **Enter your play** to record
a word directly. Refill your rack after each turn. All unseen tiles are in the
bag. Game scores, turn review, and archived games show your own plays and
score. The game finishes when you play the last tile, or you can choose
**End game** under **Game options → Manage game**.

On narrow windows, the board and rack sit above Possible moves. Wider windows put the board and moves side by side. The sidebar opens over the game as a drawer until there is room to dock it. The side-by-side layout fills the available width and reclaims the sidebar’s space when it switches to a drawer or collapses.

Use your browser’s Back and Forward buttons to return to a page, game, or selected move. Copy the address to reopen the same view. A game link ending in `?move=6` opens replay at move 6, and `?review=6` opens that turn in **Review game**. Links also keep the archive sort, Stats filters and trend metric, the saved board template you’re editing, the UI guide tab, and an installed word list selected in the checker. Search text, local files, unsaved form edits, and move analysis stay out of the address.

Monthly trends use the finish date when available, otherwise the start date. Expand **Monthly values** to see the figures and sample sizes. |
| **Game board** | The editable letter and premium grid. In live games with score tracking, a thin green border marks your turn and red marks your opponent’s. By default, the divider above the **board control strip** uses the same color. Tapping a square or selecting it with **Alt / Option + B** uses the same direction check. Tilemaxx looks right and down within the number of rack tiles and checks the squares immediately left and above for word extensions. A direct connection or multiple reachable side connections outweigh a single side connection. Ties start across. Tap the same square again to turn. Hold a placed tile to toggle blank and keep the cursor where it is. In the coordinate overlay, type **H6** or **6H** to select the same square. Adjust a typed column with **← / →** or a typed row with **↑ / ↓**. **H1** selects row 1 immediately. Type a second digit to jump to **H10** or another valid two-digit row. With your rack entered, press **Enter** while typing on the board to find moves. The **board control strip** shows the selected square as **6H** for across or **H6** for down. It contains **Define**, Undo, and Redo on desktop and phones. **Define** uses an open-book icon in a narrow strip. While typing, press **Space** before a letter to enter a blank, **.** to skip a square, or **Delete** to erase a letter. |
| **Pass and Exchange** | A divider separates these actions from **Define** in the **board control strip**. Their icons and labels are green on your turn and red on your opponent’s. Narrow strips show the icons alone. Use the player icon to switch the active player when Tilemaxx has no recorded turn to go on. **Pass** records 0 points immediately. To exchange your tiles, select them on **Your rack** and choose **Record exchange**. The remaining tiles shift left in order, and the first empty slot is ready for your replacement tiles. For an opponent exchange, choose a tile count or **Unknown**, then choose the red **Record exchange** button. Undo restores the previous turn, score history, and rack, including any replacements you typed. |
| **Last play** | The lane above the board shows the latest recorded play, with **You** or **Opp**, the main word, total points, and position when available. Hover or tap to see the points for each word formed and any bingo bonus. |
| **Board help** | The premium-square legend and board-entry instructions. |
| **Word score badge** | The points that appear beside a board word when you hover over a tile. Opponent badges have right-angle corners. Your badges have rounded corners. |
| **Definitions** | In an English or French game, hover over a move word for half a second on desktop or hold it on a phone. This works on suggestions, endgame lines, sample replies, setups, exchange examples, and the words in score breakdowns and game review. Choose **Define**, then select a highlighted tile in a word. The definition opens beside the tile on desktop and in a sheet on phones. On desktop, you can also right-click a placed tile. An intersection shows both **Across** and **Down**. |
| **Move preview** | Highlighted tiles show the previewed play on the main board and mini boards. Green marks your letters, red marks the opponent’s, and blue marks assigned blanks. In a narrow workspace, the **preview bar** shows the word, points, and **Play** button. Tap a move on a phone or right-click it on desktop to pin its preview. Tap or click outside the move and its preview controls to clear it. |
| **Your rack** | The tile editor sits below the board on desktop and beside it on phones. Drag the handle between the board and rack on phones, or between the board and Possible moves on desktop, to resize the board. The vertical rack follows the handle and stays vertically centered beside the board. The tray, tiles, and pouch shrink together to fit beside the board. This browser remembers the divider position. After you play, the first empty slot is selected for the refill if tiles remain in the bag. **Shuffle rack** sits on the left on desktop and in **Rack tools** on phones. On phones, the pouch sits below the rack. It estimates the tiles in the bag by subtracting a full opponent rack from the unseen tile count, down to zero. The pouch turns red when the tiles on the board and rack exceed the configured count for any letter or blank. Select it to see the excess in Unseen tiles. Drag a tile between its neighbors to insert it, or focus a tile and press **Shift + ← / →** on desktop or **Shift + ↑ / ↓** in the vertical rack. Rack marks follow their tiles. |
| **Rack tools** | Choose **Enter your play** to draft a word directly on the board. Use the same square, direction, and skip controls as for opponent entry. Tilemaxx uses rack letters first and fills any shortfall with blanks, shown in blue. If the rack still cannot supply a letter, the tile is outlined in amber and the board strip shows what is missing. Use **Fix rack** to correct the rack while keeping the draft, or choose **Record anyway** and confirm which tiles were left before drawing. **Play** or **Enter** records a complete play, and **Cancel** or **Escape** discards it. Use **Exclude tiles** to hold tiles back or **Require tiles** to use them in every suggested move. Move between rack tiles with **← / →** on desktop or **↑ / ↓** in the vertical rack. Long-press a tile or press **↓** on desktop or **→** in the vertical rack to cycle through excluded, required, and unmarked. Press **↑** on desktop or **←** in the vertical rack to toggle exchange. Playing clears both kinds of mark, and Undo restores them. On phones, open Rack tools from the sliders icon above the rack. |
| **Optimal exchange** | The button below your rack on desktop and in **Rack tools** on phones compares swaps while tiles remain in the bag. It requires enough playable rack tiles for a bingo under the active rules: 7 in standard Scrabble and Lexulous (whose rack holds 8). Empty slots and held tiles do not count; blank tiles do. The first pass screens swaps on the current board. Up to 20 finalists are ranked on 256 shared reply scenarios using the same opponent model as Bingo Mercenary. **Keep your rack** shows bingo availability on the current board. **Simulate exchange** shows expected score, bingo chance, and sample plays on the current board, with the after-reply estimate from Optimal exchange when available. |
| **Opponent rack** | The inferred tiles centered directly below yours when the bag is empty. On phones, the opponent rack wraps into two columns below your vertical rack. |
| **Unseen tiles** | The expandable tile counts below the racks. Open them from the bag icon or from the summary below the desktop rack. The counts include tiles in the bag and on other racks. |
| **Game scores** | The score tracking panel with totals, right-aligned opponent statistics, and a signed point difference centered beneath the scores. An inward-pointing chevron beside the active player’s total marks whose turn it is. A gold trophy marks the winner of a finished game. Archives show the final move count, average points, best play, and bingo count. During replay these stats include recorded plays through the selected move. **Find moves** records a scoreable opponent play before showing suggestions. Overwrite a placed tile or erase and re-enter it to correct its letter or blank status and update the recorded score. Use **Space** before typing a blank. On the latest opponent play recorded by **Find moves**, an erase reopens the play until the original squares are filled again or you record a different placement. To add a missing tile, use **Edit last opponent move**. Recording your pass or exchange also records a valid opponent play still on the board. Use **Record opponent move** to save a placement by itself. |
| **Game finish** | The board overlay shows the adjusted final score when either player goes out in a tracked game. On a win, fireworks continue while the overlay is open. Select **Archive game** to save it to Archived games and stay on its board. Tap or click outside the result card, or press **Escape**, to dismiss it. Select the final score in a finished or archived game to replay the finish animation. |
| **Score by move** | The chart above **Tracked move history** plots cumulative points from the current scoring period in pairs. Hover, tap, or focus the chart and use the arrow keys to see both scores and the words played. Hover over a word in the tooltip to see its definition. If a pair has one recorded play, the other score stays flat. Archives show a marker at every plotted pair. Inspect a pair on the final board to show the cursor, or scrub into the game to follow the current replay pair. |
| **Tracked move history** | Each row shows the player, turn, points, and running total. Passes and exchanges count as turns and score 0 points. Opponent rows have a gray background. The **Move strength** meter fills to the recorded points divided by the highest legal score from the saved board and rack, using the selected word list. It uses the rounded percentage for its color, with green for 0–60%, yellow for 61–80%, and red for 81–100%. Hover, focus, or tap the meter to see the percentage and points. Endgame comparisons use your saved rack as entered. A dash means there is no reliable comparison. Select a word in live play to open **Turn review**. In an archive or during replay, select a word to jump to that move. The **Review game** button above the history opens policy timelines and missed opportunities. All review analysis uses the game’s currently selected word list. Recorded moves and scores stay unchanged. |
| **Replay game** | In a live game, choose **Game options → Replay game** or use the shortcut in **Game scores**. Archives open on the final board with the replay strip visible. Scrub or press **← / →** to load the board and rack from before a recorded move. The recorded play and its drawn tiles appear automatically. On a phone, drawn tiles sit in a shelf attached to the right of the rack. Select the word in the strip to toggle its preview. Tournament notation puts reused letters in parentheses and shows blanks in lowercase. Starting squares use row first for across and column first for down. **Space** plays or pauses replay. Use **Find moves**, the filters, Define, and the rack tools on the selected position. Playing a candidate or editing the board or rack starts a temporary trial line with its own Undo and Redo. Tiles added to the board during the trial are purple. Animated tiles turn purple on takeoff. **Back to move** returns to the start of the turn. An incomplete rack can be filled in for a trial. The rack groups tiles left over before tiles used. While the bag still has tiles, grey question marks show unknown rack tiles at the end. Hover over a played word to see its definition. Use **Back to live** to leave a live replay, or scrub to the right edge to return to an archive’s final board. **Escape** also leaves the historical position. |
| **Policy timeline** | In **Review game**, select **Opponent plays** or **Your plays** and analyze that side’s recorded placements in chronological order. **Your plays** estimates fit from the opponent’s point of view using the board and your recorded placements. **Top 5 fit** shows how often the placement makes the scoring shortlist across racks that could supply the played tiles. **Random** shows the shortlist's share of legal placements. Highlights require at least 80% fit and at most 25% Random. Sampled turns check 95% bounds at 128, 256, 512, and 1,024 racks. Borderline marks turns where the bounds leave a highlight in question. Select a move to see each policy’s chance of choosing the exact placement on a rack that could supply the played tiles. Open **Raw policy estimates** to also factor in the chance of holding those tiles. |

| **Move history** | The board and rack change log in **Game options**. Undo and Redo step through these changes. |

## Possible moves and analysis

| Name | Where to find it |

| **Possible moves** | The list starts with up to 100 moves in the current sort order. Scroll past move 100 and choose **Show all moves** to reveal the remaining matches. On phones, the board and rack stay in place while you find, preview, or close moves. Select the header before running **Find moves** to collapse or expand the panel. The star runs **Find moves**, including when the panel is collapsed. The results header contains **Search possible moves**, **All moves**, **Playing style**, the move and bingo counts, and **Options**. | **Solve endgame** | Available in active and archived games whenever the current position has an empty bag, including after **Find moves**, when the button is under **Options**. Either rack can be partial. The opponent plays next. The panel ranks up to five options by swing from your perspective, worst first. The swing column includes the ply count. Hover over a row with the mouse to preview the move on the board. Click, right-click, or tap a row to toggle its selection and open or close the optimal line in **Reply details**. |
| **Move filters** | Open **Search possible moves** with the magnifying glass or the spinner while calculations continue. Type a word or a case-insensitive regular expression such as `^Q.*T$` or `/^(CAT|DOG)$/`. Use **All moves** in the header. On phones, it appears as a list icon. With **No style**, selecting **Shortlist** again returns to **All moves**. |
| **Move list** | The sortable table of suggestions. A **move row** is one suggestion, and a **column header** controls sorting. Open **WORD** and choose **Word length** to put the longest completed words first, or **Tiles used** to put plays that use the most rack tiles first. Both break ties by higher points. The small number at the upper left of each row is its position in the full list sorted by raw points. It stays fixed across other sorts and filters. PTS is the play’s score. The smaller number underneath is the gap from the best score available with all rack marks cleared. **Adj.** is the score minus the reply delta. The list sorts by adjusted points once the initial reply checks finish. Choose either order in the points header. It reads **ADJ. PTS** when sorting by adjusted points. **Playing style** samples suggestions from the moves the Casual, Learner, or Strong profile could spot. Choose **Resample style** under **Options** to get a fresh set of suggestions from that profile. The row shows its sampled rank. In two-player games, **Learner** checks the reply and the next turn for leading moves when tiles remain in the bag. Hover or select the points cell for the style breakdown. This browser remembers the style for each game. Choose **Bingo Mercenary** for current points and expected next-turn bingo points. Choose **Bingo Tactician** to also account for the opponent’s reply, ordinary follow-ups, and bingo lanes. Ordinary plays contribute 75% of their current score and immediate bingos contribute 100%. **Run Bingo Mercenary** and **Run Bingo Tactician** start an analysis for up to 10 seconds. The move and bingo counts use the same totals in No style and all playing styles. Once used in a game, the selected style starts automatically on new **Find moves** searches. **Run another 10 seconds** continues provisional results. Bingo Mercenary also checks **High-ceiling setups** after its sampled ranking is ready. **U** shows the simulated styles’ utility below the points and points gap. Hover or select the points cell for its utility breakdown and rank. **Swap** appears in the header when the selected style would exchange. It marks the tiles and opens **Simulate exchange**, with the style’s reason. **No style** clears the saved style. **All moves** opens the full list and keeps your playing style saved. [Playing styles](player-profiles.md). |
| **Playing style** | In solo games, choose **Greedy Bingo** for immediate and next-turn bingos, **Score Lookahead** for points now and expected points over your next two turns, or **Bingo & Triple Words** for immediate bingos, current points, next-turn bingo chance, and open triple-word lanes. Casual, Learner, and Strong also rank solo plays using your board and rack. In two-player games, selecting **Master** automatically compares the leading scores and leaves through the opponent’s reply and your next turn. Open **Utility breakdown** on a move to see the estimate or the swing from a known-rack endgame. |
| **Run another 10 seconds** | Appears below the endgame status when any of the six automatic exact lines reaches a limit. Continues only unfinished lines, allowing up to ten more seconds per move and reusing retained search work. It remains available after further limits. Changing the position, reply settings, worker limit, or reloading releases unfinished search state. Exact line searches use at most two workers to bound retained memory. |
| **Calculation timing** | Select the time below the move list for a breakdown. **All moves** shows the initial **Find moves** work, including its reply histograms and exact endgame solves when available. **Shortlist** and playing styles show only their own ranking, simulations, and additional reply histograms. Completed simulation slices contribute to the same view. The total counts overlapping work once and excludes gaps between runs. |
| **Reply delta** | The move-list number showing the change in the opponent’s reply score against the reference move. A lower opponent reply is favorable. |
| **Estimated endgame swing** | The EST. SWING column when the bag is empty. The figure measures the change in your lead. A fast search considers four promising moves per turn, finishing moves, and passes, looking up to seven plays ahead. These selective results remain approximate. The top six estimates receive automatic exact searches, whose completed results replace the estimates. It may also name an opponent go-out threat. |
| **Reply details** | The box at the bottom of Possible moves headed **Reply after [move]**. With tiles in the bag, Tilemaxx automatically checks the top 20 moves by score plus up to two **Premium block** and two **Better leave** candidates. When you select a playing style, Tilemaxx also calculates missing reply histograms for its top 20 ranked moves. Use the chart icon in the reply column to **Check reply histogram** for another move. The details show the distribution, comparison, chance the reply outscores your play, and **Heatmap**. With an empty bag they show the opponent rack, immediate reply, score checkpoints, and the option to calculate the optimal endgame line when applicable. |
| **Reply settings** | The expandable bag count and endgame controls. It shows **Prior scoreless turns** for Scrabble and Words With Friends, **Prior consecutive passes** for Wordfeud and Lexulous, and **Opponent has a final turn** for Crossplay. |
| **Reply heatmap** | The board view of where strong replies land. Select a **hotspot** to see its change and sample replies. |
| **Reply risk** | The quick badge shows the score of an opened premium reply and the chance the opponent holds its tiles. Rare examples are muted. Strong red marks replies that are both likely and costly. Tap the badge for the example and its before-and-after scores. |
| **Bingo odds** | The draw and board-based odds in the move list, with controls to calculate the board-based chances. |
| **Move analysis** | Open **Analyze** under **Options**. Pick **analysis candidates** and choose **Run analysis**. Use **Opponent model** to view predictions for a [playing style](player-profiles.md). With an empty bag, adjust the **Endgame budget** to give each exact search more work. |
| **Move comparison** | The metric table in Move analysis. The first candidate is the **reference move** for relative comparisons. |
| **Move analysis details** | The expandable card for an individual candidate beneath Move comparison. It can show example replies, endgame swing, and final score. |

## Move notation

Move locations encode direction: `8G` starts across at column G, row 8; `G8` starts down at the same square. Board squares keep their letter-first labels, such as `G8`.

In **Game scores → Tracked move history**, lowercase letters are blanks and parentheses group letters already on the board: `C(A)tS` places C, a blank T, and S around an existing A. Existing blanks stay lowercase inside parentheses. Other word displays keep their usual spelling and tile colors; bingo trophies remain visible. Recorded moves without enough saved placement information retain their recorded spelling.
